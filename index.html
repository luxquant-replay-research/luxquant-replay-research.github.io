How to read this

Claim is not a price record. It is the exit implied by the provider’s published status (tp1…tp3, closed_win, closed_loss). A status that says a target was touched does not mean the position was closed there, nor that it stayed profitable afterwards.

Real Replay Market re-reads the actual 1-minute path from publication to the present: entry is filled only when price touches the published entry, the stop stays at stop1 unless a different model is chosen, and the trade ends at the stop or the final target. Position boxes on the charts start one minute after publication (assumed execution delay).

Why the models differ. The same candles are replayed under four stop rules: hold to final, move to entry after TP1, move to TP1 after TP1, and move SL to TP1 after TP2. The spread between them is the value of stop management, not a different set of trades.

Limits. Intrabar order is unknown. When a candle touches both the stop and a target the replay refuses to guess and marks the signal ambiguous or unavailable. Symbols that are delisted, renamed, or never listed on Binance USD-M are excluded from the replay side and shown honestly. Replayed R includes Binance USD-M maker 0.02% entry and taker 0.05% exit fees; funding is not applied. Claim R is a gross price ratio and therefore reads slightly higher on the same target. This is research, not trading advice, and a past touch is not evidence of a profitable trade.

Claim = the exit implied by the provider’s published status (exit at the touched target, or −1R if reported loss). Real Replay Market = the same signal replayed on Binance USD-M 1-minute candles: entry on touch, stop stays at SL1, position held to the final target unless a different model is selected.

Costs. Every replayed R uses the app’s Binance USD-M cost model: maker 0.02% on the limit entry and taker 0.05% on each exit; funding is not applied. Claim R is the gross price ratio with no costs, so on the same target it is slightly higher than the replayed R (for example a full-target trade can read +4.03R gross versus +3.91R after fees).

Verdict compares the claim with the observed path: CONFLICT (target claimed but stop hit first, or loss claimed although a target was reached), PARTIAL (target reached, then stopped), MATCH (claim matches), OPEN (unresolved).

The R columns are the same candles and entries under different stop management: hold to final keeps the stop at SL1; move to entry after TP1 sets the stop to the entry (breakeven); move to tp1 after tp1 sets the stop to TP1; move sl to tp1 after tp2 sets the stop to TP1 only once TP2 is actually reached, otherwise the stop stays at SL1. A “—” means the trade is still open. The Exit-management scenarios cards pair each setting with the claim measured on the same signals that resolved under that setting, so the claim and the replay there are directly comparable; counts can differ slightly between cards because a setting may resolve a signal that another leaves open.

In the chart view the entry is drawn as a position box: green = entry → TP4 and red = entry → SL1; the model chips show/hide each scenario. Boxes start on the first candle one minute after publication (assumed 1-minute execution delay).

Charts use TradingView Lightweight Charts with public Binance USD-M Futures candles. Entry, TP1–TP4 and stop1 are drawn as levels; markers show the entry touch and each exit. This is independent research, not trading advice.
